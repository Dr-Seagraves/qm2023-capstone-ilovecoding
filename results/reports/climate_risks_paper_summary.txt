
SUMMARY: Climate Risks Paper (SSRN-3795964)
================================================================================

TITLE: Dissecting Climate Risks: Are they Reflected in Stock Prices?
AUTHORS: Faccini, Matin & Skiadopoulos (2023)
PAGES: 70

KEY FINDINGS FROM ABSTRACT:
- Studies period: January 2000 - December 2018
- Uses textual analysis of Reuters climate-change news
- Identifies 4 novel risk factors:
  1. Natural Disasters (Physical Risk)
  2. Global Warming (Physical Risk)  
  3. International Summits (Transition Risk)
  4. U.S. Climate Policy (Transition Risk)

KEY RESULT: Only climate-policy factor is priced (especially post-2012)
INTERPRETATION: Investors hedge TRANSITION risks from government intervention
              rather than direct physical risks from climate change

RELEVANCE TO REIT ANALYSIS:
================================================================================
1. REITs are highly sensitive to climate risks:
   - Physical: Property damage from natural disasters
   - Transition: Regulatory changes, carbon taxes, green building requirements

2. Climate policy affects interest rates:
   - Government intervention → monetary policy response
   - Carbon pricing → inflation expectations → rate changes
   - Green transition → capital flows → interest rate dynamics

3. Lagged effects in REITs:
   - Policy announcements → gradual implementation
   - Market adjustment takes time (information diffusion)
   - Real estate responses lag due to transaction costs
   - Similar to paper's finding of post-2012 pricing

METHODOLOGY TO ADAPT:
================================================================================
1. Textual analysis approach (LDA) for risk factor identification
2. Time-varying factor loadings
3. Portfolio sorts to test pricing
4. Cross-sectional tests with control variables
5. Examination of lagged and persistent effects

APPLICATION TO OUR ANALYSIS:
================================================================================
- Examine how climate/policy shocks affect REIT returns over time
- Test for lagged effects similar to interest rate changes
- Consider both immediate and delayed responses
- Account for regime changes (e.g., post-2012 in their paper)
